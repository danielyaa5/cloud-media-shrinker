Cloud Media Shrinker Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 6, 2026

Cloud Media Shrinker is a Mac, iPhone and iPad app by Daniel Yakobian. It helps you review and compress photos and videos stored in Apple Photos or folders you choose.

Your media stays under your control

Cloud Media Shrinker processes photos and videos on your device. The app does not automatically send your photos, videos, filenames, captions, location information or library contents to the developer. You can choose to share information in a support report. The app has no advertising, tracking or analytics SDKs and does not require a Cloud Media Shrinker account.

Permissions

Apple Photos access lets the app read your library, create compressed copies and remove items you select. The app requests permission before accessing Photos. Your Mac or iPhone asks for confirmation when removing Photos items.

To measure sizes without downloading originals on older macOS versions, you can select your Photos library. Cloud Media Shrinker reads its local size records through a read-only database connection and remembers the permission with a read-only bookmark. It does not modify the Photos database.

Folder access is limited to the locations you choose. The Mac App Store edition and the iPhone app store security-scoped bookmarks locally so they can reopen those locations. On a Mac, Photos automation permission is used to copy titles, captions and keywords, and to show selected items in Photos.

iCloud and Google Drive

Apple Photos may download media from iCloud when you preview or compress it. On a Mac, Google Drive support uses Google Drive for desktop and folders you choose in Finder. The iPhone app does not read Google Drive. Google Drive handles its own downloads, uploads, account information and cloud storage under Google's policies. Cloud Media Shrinker does not request your Google password or connect directly to your Google account.

Information saved on your device

The app saves settings, folder locations, a library measurement cache and a history of compression and deletion operations on your device. This history is not shared between your devices. These can include filenames, item identifiers, file sizes and processing settings. They are not sent to the developer. The free-compression count is also saved in the device's Keychain so reinstalling does not reset the allowance. Removing a folder from the app's list does not delete its files.

Network connections and updates

On a Mac, the interface communicates with the app through a server bound only to that Mac's loopback address. The iPhone app opens no server. The Mac App Store edition receives updates through the App Store and does not check GitHub for updates. The website download checks GitHub for newer releases unless you disable automatic checks. GitHub receives the normal connection information needed to answer that request, such as your IP address; no media or library information is included.

The project website is hosted separately and uses a visit counter. Its hosting and counter services may process website traffic information. The counter is not included in the app.

Support reports

On a Mac or iPhone, when you choose Send in the app's bug report or feedback form, the app sends the text you entered, your optional reply email, selected screenshots, and app diagnostics if included, to support@danielyakobian.com through a Cloudflare-hosted relay and Resend. Opening the form does not send a report. These services receive connection information needed to process the request. The relay does not store report contents; Resend and the support mailbox process and retain the email.

On iPhone, Email Support instead opens a message in your mail app with the app and iOS versions and recent structured app events already filled in, and nothing is sent unless you send it. Diagnostics contain app and system versions, architecture, distribution channel, session duration and recent structured app events. They exclude media filenames, library paths, Photos identifiers, account details and library history. You can review the exact diagnostics and exclude them before sending. Screenshot metadata is removed, but anything visible in a screenshot is shared. To request removal of a support report, contact support@danielyakobian.com with its reference.

Support and deletion

If you choose to report a problem through GitHub or email, the information you provide is handled by the service you use. Do not include private media or library history unless you intend to share it. Contact the developer or report a privacy concern through the project's support page.

You can revoke Photos and Automation access in System Settings on a Mac, or Photos access in Settings on iPhone. You can remove local app storage from the app's support folder on a Mac, or by deleting the app on iPhone or iPad. The free-compression count in the device's Keychain may remain after reinstalling. Removing app storage does not remove your original or compressed media. The developer has no server copy of your library to delete.
